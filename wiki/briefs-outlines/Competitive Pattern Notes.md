# Competitive Pattern Notes

## Competitive Pattern Notes Observation Job

This note records what the visible SERP and competitor set appear to emphasize, then translates those observations into a better brief without copying another publisher. The deliverable is an observation sheet with evidence state, not a list of ranking factors. Keep screenshots, dates, locale, device, and query variants in the project brief when available.

Competitive patterns matter because they reveal reader expectations, source types, media formats, answer depth, and gaps. Use `dfs-api` or manual dated SERP capture for live-result facts, and `dfs-labs` when overlap datasets shape the pattern. `g-helpful-content` keeps copied formats subordinate to people-first value. Use `g-ai-opt-guide` when a competitor promotes special AI files or unsupported Google AI tactics.

### What To Record

Record page types, answer formats, source categories, freshness signals, media elements, author or organization trust signals, and missing reader steps. A good observation says "the current results use comparison tables and dated screenshots" rather than "Google rewards tables."

### What Not To Copy

Do not replicate a competitor outline, invented statistic, visual sequence, or claim stack. The brief should use observations to create original value, clearer evidence, or a better reader path.

## Gap To Outline Procedure

1. Separate each observed pattern into format, source, media, freshness, or trust signal.
2. Decide whether the pattern answers a reader need or merely reflects competitor habit.
3. Convert useful patterns into brief requirements with original examples, stronger evidence, or clearer decision support.
4. Send unsupported factual gaps to [[Evidence Block Requirements]] before the outline is approved.
5. Store dated SERP details in [[SERP Observation Ledger]] when the pattern may need future rollback.

## Evidence Limits And Caveats

SERP observations are volatile. A single live result can justify a brief question, but it cannot prove causation. Treat competitor claims as claims to verify, not source material. When the pattern relates to AI exposure or low-click behavior, link the caveat to [[AI Citation Mechanics]] or [[Dual Optimization]] instead of repeating broad statistics here.
