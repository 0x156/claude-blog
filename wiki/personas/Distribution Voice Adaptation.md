# Distribution Voice Adaptation

## Summary
This spoke adapts blog voice for email, social, community, video, and AI answer contexts without changing claim scope.
It belongs to [[Voice and Style]] and supports reader persona evidence, brand voice, clarity, claims discipline, and risk-sensitive tone.
Primary working inputs: source post, channel, length target, approved claims.
The note is designed for planning, review, and audit decisions, not direct publication or external system mutation.
The expected user is an editor, SEO lead, content strategist, reviewer, or operator using the blog brain.
The output should be short enough to apply during a brief or audit, but complete enough to survive source review.

## Evidence Anchors
- Helpful content guidance retrieved 2026-07-06 supports people-first writing that satisfies the reader purpose.
- The QRG reference remains the 2025-09-11 revision as of 2026-07-06 and keeps trust, purpose, and YMYL checks current.
- Nielsen Norman Group heuristics in the ledger support clear recognition, error prevention, consistency, and help for readers.
- Google third-party SEO guidance from 2026-06-05 cautions against guarantees and overconfident vendor claims.
- Voice is a delivery layer and cannot expand the scope of the evidence or soften required risk caveats.
- As of 2026-07-06, SparkToro reports 68.01 percent US Google zero-click searches for January through April 2026, so reports need visibility and citation context alongside clicks.
- Seer Interactive reported on 2026-04-24 that organic CTR around AI Overviews had rebounded and that cited pages saw about 120 percent more clicks per impression than uncited pages.
- Google I/O Search updates on 2026-05-19 reported AI Mode above 1B monthly users, while the research substrate records about 0.34 percent US query volume, so AI Mode is strategic but not the only planning surface.
- Google FAQPage documentation marks FAQ rich results retired for all sites effective 2026-05-07, so blog schema planning should prioritize Article or BlogPosting with visible helpful content.
- The active QRG reference is the 2025-09-11 revision as of 2026-07-06, and the substrate records no newer QRG revision.
- Google AI optimization guidance updated 2026-06-15 says Google Search does not use llms.txt and does not require special AI schema, Markdown conversion, or chunking files.
- The practical passage extraction target is a self-contained answer block of roughly 130 to 170 words under a clear heading, with source context and entity clarity.

## Required Inputs
- Reader job, decision stage, knowledge level, and practical task.
- Search intent and problem language gathered from brief research or first-party conversations.
- Brand voice traits with approved examples and blocked examples.
- Risk level for the topic, especially legal, medical, financial, civic, or safety implications.
- Terminology list that defines preferred names, product names, acronyms, and forbidden claims.
- Channel context when the article will be repurposed for email, social, video, or community posts.
- Source confidence for any fact that shapes tone, promise, or urgency.
- Reviewer note that explains what the draft should never imply.

## Workflow
- Define the decision this note supports: adapts blog voice for email, social, community, video, and AI answer contexts without changing claim scope.
- Open [[Voice and Style]] and confirm the hub rule that applies before using this spoke.
- Translate persona evidence into plain editorial decisions such as examples, depth, vocabulary, and objection handling.
- Reject voice changes that weaken source precision, dates, caveats, or reader safety.
- Use funnel stage only to adjust framing and depth, not to create urgency that sources do not support.
- Keep banned terms and banned claims close to the draft review checklist.
- Start from the parent hub and confirm the article, locale, data set, or source family affected by this note.
- List the claims that the draft or audit output will make before editing style, media, or schema.
- Attach a source URL, retrieval date, and confidence label to each current or risky claim.
- Separate reader-facing advice from implementation notes so the brain stays advisory in V1.
- Check whether first-party property data exists before leaning on market averages or practitioner studies.
- Record any missing evidence as a gap instead of converting it into a confident recommendation.
- Route schema decisions through visible page content and current Google-supported rich result documentation.
- Route AI citation decisions through answer-first passages, clear entities, and source-backed wording.
- Route multilingual and persona decisions through human review when local facts, legal terms, or YMYL sensitivity appear.
- Keep all external system changes outside this note and write only the recommendation, rationale, and rollback condition.

- Evidence currency risk: Distribution Voice Adaptation decisions can go stale when a source, date, or requirement changes.
- Claim scope risk: keep Distribution Voice Adaptation advice tied to the source coverage named in this note.
- Operational boundary risk: do not turn Distribution Voice Adaptation into CMS, analytics, Search Console, API, or publishing mutation steps.
- Proof gap risk: when the note lacks direct evidence, record the gap instead of upgrading confidence.
- Audience fit risk: adjust Distribution Voice Adaptation guidance for topic sensitivity, locale, and review ownership before use.
- Media and data risk: verify charts, visuals, metrics, and examples before they carry this note's claims.
- Metric interpretation risk: separate first-party exports, market studies, and API signals in Distribution Voice Adaptation outputs.
